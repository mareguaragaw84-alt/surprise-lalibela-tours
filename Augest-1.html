Ethiopia in August

At the beginning of August, the rains are at their most intense. Early August is the worst time to visit the country, but the rains begin to calm as you edge closer to September, and the country is incredibly beautiful at this time of year with the flowers in full bloom. Marc, Brilliant Ethiopia. Is August a good month to visit Ethiopia? August sees the worst of the rainy season, with the heaviest downpours. Some lodges and campsites in the national parks will be closed, which limits how much of the country you can see. There will also likely be disruptions in travel connections around the country as roads are bogged down and heavy rains disrupt domestic flight schedules. The Omo Valley is a good place to be at this time of year as the rains have largely left this region, and community trekking in the Gheralta Mountains is very special in August. To learn more, check out our guide: when is the best time to go to Ethiopia? Ethiopia in AugustAdmiring the view en route to Erar, Community Trekking.

You start your adventure in the bustling capital city of Ethiopia, Addis Ababa. Most international flights arrive in the morning at Addis, so we'll collect you from the airport and take you to your hotel where you can relax and freshen up after your flight. In the afternoon you can take your first steps into the city and explore some of the sites in Addis, before heading to one of the capital's wonderful cultural restaurants. From Addis, you head to Bahir Dar, a charming lakeside town that's great for some light adventuring. You can take a boat trip out onto Lake Tana and the visit the Blue Nile Falls. Next, head to Lalibela, one of Ethiopia's most famous destinations home to the incredible rock-hewn churches and the fantastic Ben Abeba restaurant. You then head to Gonder, an ancient capital city of Ethiopia and home to the crumbling castle ruins of ancient kings and queens - Gonder is known as the Camelot of Africa. From Gondar, the road leads up into the spectacular beauty of the Simien Mountains National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site and a place of almost unimaginable beauty. There are 3 main campsites in the Simiens: Sankaber, Geech, and Chennek. The classic trail starts by the park entrance and heads to Sankaber for the first night. You then trek on to Geech for a second night, and a final night at Chennek (the best of the 3 campsites with exceptional views, and the best place to spot Walia Ibex!). If you don't have time for the full 3 night trek, you can skip one of the campsites to make it 2 nights - or even one! After camping in the mountains, close out your time in the Simiens at the stunning Limalimo Lodge overlooking the beautiful Simien range. Limalimo is one of the best properties in Ethiopia, combining incredible views with beautiful design and unmatched service. The perfect spot for sundowners by the roaring fire after a few days exploring! After the Simiens, take a drive down the switchbacks to Axum, the original capital city of Ethiopia. Axum was once a hugely influential city back in the days of the Axumite empire. Its glory days are long gone, but the relics that remain from that time are fascinating and its one of the most culturally significant regions of Ethiopia. The drive passes through incredible scenery.
